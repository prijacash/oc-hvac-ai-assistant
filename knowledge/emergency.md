# Emergency policy

Fictional demo document for Orange County HVAC Techs. The company is not real. The safety steps for gas and fire are the ones the assistant must give. The shop phone number is fictional. The gas-utility number below is the published Southern California Gas Company emergency line, included so the demo does not invent an emergency number.

## Gas smell or carbon monoxide

If you smell gas, notice a gas odor or a rotten-egg smell, hear hissing at a gas line, or a carbon monoxide alarm is sounding, that is not a wait-for-the-HVAC-tech situation.

1. Leave the building immediately. Do not hunt for the source.
2. Do not flip switches, light flames, or use a phone until you are outside.
3. From outside, call 911.
4. Then call the Southern California Gas Company emergency line at 1-800-427-2200, or the emergency number printed on the gas bill.
5. Do not wait for Orange County HVAC Techs. We will not dispatch a technician as the first response to a suspected gas leak or carbon monoxide alarm.

The assistant may log a note so the office has a record, but that log is not a dispatch and not an appointment confirmation.

## No heat or no cooling in unsafe temperatures

No heat, or no cooling, is an emergency when the home is unsafe: a heat wave, freezing weather, or anyone in the home who is an infant, elderly, or medically sensitive to temperature.

- Call (714) 555-0198 and say it is an emergency no-heat or no-cooling call.
- We flag these for same-day emergency dispatch.
- Outside posted hours, the $149 after-hours trip charge applies and is not waived.
- This is not automatically a 911 call. If someone is in medical distress, has symptoms of heat stroke, or is not responding, call 911 first.

A warm house on a mild day, or an air conditioner that is noisy but still cooling, is not this emergency. Those can be booked as soon or routine.

## Fire, smoke, or sparking

If there is fire, heavy smoke, or sparking that will not stop, call 911. If it is safe, turn the system off at the thermostat and the breaker. A burning electrical smell with no fire is a reason to shut the system off and call (714) 555-0198, not to keep running it.

## Water from the air handler

Water leaking from the indoor unit is usually a clogged condensate line or a failed pan. Turn the system off so it does not keep overflowing, and call us. It is not a 911 situation by itself.
